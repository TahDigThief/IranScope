# The IRGC's assassination and kidnapping plots on foreign soil

The islamic republic does not just kill its critics inside Iran. For decades it has sent operatives, hired criminals, and used diplomats to hunt down dissidents, journalists, and former officials in other countries. Here are some documented cases.

## Findings

- Iran's first IRGC minister admitted in a 2026 interview that he personally oversaw the assassinations of exiled opposition figures abroad, including former Prime Minister Shapour Bakhtiar, killed in Paris in 1991, and said the regime paid a Basque separatist group to carry out some of the killings. ([source](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202503093512))
- The IRGC tried for years to kidnap journalist Masih Alinejad from her home in Brooklyn and smuggle her back to Iran. After the kidnapping plots failed, the IRGC hired Russian mob leaders, who paid a New York man to track and shoot her in the street. He was carrying a loaded AK-47 when he was arrested. ([source](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/two-russian-mob-leaders-sentenced-25-years-prison-murder-hire-targeting-journalist-behalf))
- When that plot also failed, the IRGC tried again in 2024, paying a man already convicted of murder $100,000 to find and kill Alinejad, an American citizen, in New York. ([source](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/brooklyn-man-sentenced-15-years-prison-murder-hire-plot-and-stalking-journalist-and))
- Jamshid Sharmahd, a German-Iranian activist, was kidnapped while traveling abroad in 2020, forcibly taken to Iran, and sentenced to death after a sham trial. ([source](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy2052))
- Journalist Ruhollah Zam was lured to Iraq in 2019, kidnapped by the IRGC, brought back to Iran, tortured, and executed in December 2020. ([source](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy2052))
- A Dutch-Ahwazi activist, Ahmad Molla Nissi, was assassinated in The Hague in 2017 as part of the same transnational network the US and UK later sanctioned together. ([source](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy2052))
- An IRGC operative in the US planned to murder dissident Amir Fakhravar's wife and child in front of him before killing Fakhravar himself. ([source](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202503093512))
- In 2022, Iran nearly succeeded in assassinating Mike Pompeo at a hotel in Paris, intelligence officials later revealed. ([source](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2025/06/27/trump-iran-pompeo-assassination/))
- That same year, the IRGC's Shahram Poursafi was indicted by the DOJ for plotting to assassinate John Bolton and offering up to $1 million to kill Pompeo. ([source](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy1513))
- Belgian authorities stopped an Iranian diplomat from personally delivering a bomb meant for a gathering of Iranian dissidents in France in 2018. He was later convicted in a Belgian court. ([source](https://freedomhouse.org/report/transnational-repression/iran))
- MI5 has stated that Iranian intelligence services, including the IRGC, increasingly use criminal proxies in the UK to carry out attacks while staying hidden in Tehran. ([source](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202503093512))
- The regime also goes after dissidents' families left behind in Iran: Masih Alinejad's brother was sentenced to eight years in prison and her sister was forced to denounce her on state television, both to pressure her into silence. ([source](https://freedomhouse.org/report/transnational-repression/iran))
- Iran's campaign of foreign assassinations has continued for over four decades (i.e. the entire time the islamic republic has existed), starting with the killing of an Iranian Navy officer in Paris in 1979.
