# The islamic republic tortured a Canadian photojournalist to death, then blamed her for her own murder

Zahra Kazemi was a 54 year old Iranian-Canadian photojournalist who had covered conflicts across the world, from Afghanistan to Iraq. In June 2003 she went to photograph families gathered outside Tehran's Evin prison, hoping to learn what happened to relatives arrested in student protests. A prison guard told her photography was forbidden there and demanded her camera. She exposed her film to protect the families she had already photographed and handed over the empty camera, but was arrested anyway. Eighteen days later she was dead.

## Findings

- Kazemi's autopsy showed she had been raped and tortured by regime officials while held at Evin Prison.
- A former military doctor who examined her body said she had a fractured skull, a broken nose, signs of rape, and severe abdominal bruising, and fled Iran to give this account. He said her head and face looked like they had been run over by a truck.
- The regime's story changed repeatedly. Officials first claimed she suffered a stroke during interrogation, then said she fell and hit her head, before the Iranian vice president eventually admitted she died from a fractured skull caused by a blow to the head.
- The islamic republic refused to send her body back to Canada for independent examination and buried her in Shiraz against her family's wishes.
- Kazemi was detained by order of Tehran's chief prosecutor Saeed Mortazavi, a judiciary official already implicated in numerous other human rights violations.
- A single low ranking intelligence agent was put on trial for her killing and acquitted for lack of evidence. No one has ever been convicted for her death.
- The parliamentary investigation named Mortazavi as the prime suspect, but the individual who actually delivered the fatal blow was never identified or prosecuted.
- When Kazemi's mother testified that she had seen torture marks on her daughter's body before burial, the presiding judge refused every request by the family's lawyers to summon the officials, judges, and prison staff who could shed light on what happened.
- The regime also blocked Canadian government observers from attending the trial, despite earlier promises to allow it.
- Mortazavi, the man Canada holds directly responsible for Kazemi's arrest, torture and death, was never punished for it. He went on to be involved in the 2009 Kahrizak prison deaths of protesters, received only a brief sentence for that, and was later pardoned outright by Khamenei on separate corruption charges.
- Kazemi is not an isolated case. Kavous Seyed-Emami, another Iranian-Canadian, died in Evin prison in 2018 just two weeks after his arrest, in circumstances his family also rejects as the official "suicide" story.
