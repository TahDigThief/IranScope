# The islamic republic's cyber police tortured a blogger to death for posts against the regime

## TL;DR

- A man wrote a blog almost nobody read called "My Life for My Iran".
- The islamic republic tortured him to death for it in four days, then let his killer walk free by relabeling his murder as something lesser.
- His family members were threatened with arrest if they spoke to the media about the case, but his mother is still demanding justice.

Sattar Beheshti was a 35 year old laborer from Robat Karim, a working class suburb of Tehran. He ran a small blog called "My Life for My Iran" where he wrote about political and social problems in the country. Almost nobody read it but that didn't matter to the islamic republic.

On October 30, 2012, agents believed to be from the regime's Cyber Police showed up at his home and took him away. His family had no further contact with him until they got a call telling them to collect his body.

## Findings

- Beheshti was arrested by the regime's cyber police on charges of "actions against national security on social networks and Facebook". Before his arrest he had written on his blog that he had been threatened that his mother would have to wear black because he wouldn't stop speaking out.
- He spent one night in Tehran's Evin prison, where he filed an official complaint alleging he had been mistreated, before being moved to an unknown location.
- In that complaint, later made public, he said he had been arrested without a warrant and that his interrogators tortured him, including tying him to a table and kicking him in the head. Media reports said that at Evin Prison he had injuries consistent with having been hung by his wrists from the ceiling.
- Other reports described the torture he suffered as including being hung from the ceiling by different parts of his body.
- According to his family, he had been in good health before his arrest and detention. Four days after he was taken, he was dead.
- He died under torture by his interrogator on November 3, 2012. A medical report in the case file stated he suffered hemorrhaging in his liver, lungs, and cerebellum, which cut off oxygen to different parts of his body, and he slowly died as a result.
- His family was never allowed to grieve in peace. Family members were threatened with arrest if they spoke to the media about the case.
- The international backlash forced a token gesture. Tehran's cyber police chief was sacked for negligence, and judiciary officials said the investigation had led to seven arrests. The head of Iran's Cyber Police unit was dismissed, but even that gesture was quickly undermined, as a member of parliament's National Security and Foreign Policy Commission later denied the removal had anything to do with the blogger's death.
- In the end, nobody was held accountable for killing him. A Tehran court closed the case against the interrogator accused of murdering Beheshti, ruling it was not murder but "quasi-murder".
- His mother, Gohar Eshghi, refused to accept this. She told the International Campaign for Human Rights in Iran she was protesting the court's decision to dismiss the murder charges, and said she did not believe the "quasi-murder" ruling and considered it ridiculous. She pointed out that the case file already contained medical records and her son's interrogator's own confession about killing him.
- Even Iran's own president at the time admitted the state had done something wrong. Mahmoud Ahmadinejad later described a national security council session after Beheshti's death, saying he shouted at the head of the judiciary and told him "you committed a crime".
- The US government condemned the killing outright. The State Department said it was appalled by reports that Iranian authorities tortured and killed Beheshti during a prison interrogation, and pointed out he had been arrested for nothing more than expressing his political opinion online.
