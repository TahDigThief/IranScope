# The islamic republic kidnaps foreign passport holders and trades them like currency

For decades, the islamic republic has arrested people with foreign or dual citizenship on fake charges, only to hold them for years, then trade them for money, prisoners, or political concessions. Human rights researchers and even the UN have a name for it: hostage diplomacy.

## Findings

- The practice goes back to the 1979 US embassy takeover, when the islamic republic authorities held 52 American diplomats and staff for 444 days. Academic researchers call this the regime's founding act of hostage taking and the template for everything that followed.
- A 2022 study published in the Journal of International Criminal Justice documented at least 66 foreign and dual nationals detained by the islamic republic since 2010 alone, used as leverage over their other country of citizenship.
- Siamak Namazi, an Iranian American businessman, was locked in Evin Prison for almost 8 years on spying charges with no real evidence. His elderly father Baquer was arrested too, when he flew to Iran just to visit his son. Authorities refused to let Baquer leave the country for surgery on a 95 to 97 percent blocked artery, holding his health hostage as well.
- Namazi was only released in 2023 as part of a deal where the US let the regime access 6 billion dollars in frozen oil money and released five Iranian prisoners in exchange.
- Nazanin Zaghari-Ratcliffe, a British Iranian aid worker, was arrested in 2016 while visiting family with her 2 year old daughter and accused of plotting to overthrow the government. She was held for 6 years. Her husband says regime officials told her directly that her freedom depended on the UK paying off a 400 million plus pound arms debt from before the 1979 revolution. She was only released after the UK paid it.
- French citizens Cecile Kohler and Jacques Paris have been held since May 2022 without ever being convicted of an actual recognized crime. During a phone call, Kohler told her family she could not take it anymore.
- The UN Working Group on Arbitrary Detention has repeatedly ruled that these detentions are arbitrary and has called for the release of the people involved, but the islamic republic keeps doing it anyway.
- According to former French hostage Louis Arnaud, who spent 2 years in Iranian custody, detainees are usually kept in solitary confinement specifically to strip away their sense of being human.
- Human rights groups and former detainees warn that every prisoner swap the islamic republic gets away with only teaches it that hostage taking works, which is why the practice has not stopped after more than 4 decades.
