# How to stay safe from the islamic republic online if you're an activist or a journalist

The islamic republic tracks its critics online, inside and outside Iran. It monitors social media, hacks accounts, and has jailed and killed people over posts as small as a blog nobody read. The following are some safety tips, but please note that they are by no means "all you need" or "100% foolproof", they're just a good starting point.

Also, please note that these points mostly apply to Iranians living inside Iran, where the risk of being terrorized by the regime is much higher for peaceful activists and journalists (but still, they're good practices to follow no matter where you live).

First and foremost: do your own research. This is the most vital rule of cybersecurity. Never trust a single source, and instead, look up every single thing you plan on doing, including things mentioned in this post you're reading right now. Don't just take anyone's word for anything this serious, including mine, so please research each one of these tips independently before applying it.

## Tips

- Don't use your real name, real photo, real phone number, or any other real contact detail on any account or platform where you criticize the regime. Don't link that account to accounts that use your real identity under any circumstance.
- Turn on two-factor authentication everywhere instead of SMS verification codes, using an app like Google Authenticator. The regime has direct access to telecom infrastructure and has deactivated the SIM cards of activists and journalists as punishment for online posts, so don't rely on your phone number as a stable identifier or your only login method. This isn't rare or historical, it's happened repeatedly through 2024, 2025, and into 2026, including forcing people to post pro-regime content to get reconnected.
- Do not use Telegram, WhatsApp, or any other messaging app that does not use end-to-end encryption. Iran-specific messaging apps (e.g. Bale, Rubika, Eitaa, etc) are much worse because they will immediately hand your data to the regime if asked to. Instead, rely on apps that encrypt messages end to end and store the least data if they're ever compelled to hand something over (the Signal app is one example).
- Use a VPN at all times, but pick one with a strict no-logs policy that has been independently audited. A bad or fake VPN can be worse than no VPN at all. Avoid all Iranian VPNs, as they will immediately hand your personal information to the regime when asked to.
- Never click links sent to you out of the blue, even from people who seem to be friends or fellow activists. Phishing pages that look like Gmail or Instagram login pages are one of the most common ways accounts get taken over.
- Use a password manager and make every password unique. Reused passwords mean one leaked account can unlock all your others. Plus, using a password manager allows you to use long, randomly-generated, complex passwords without having to memorize them.
- Assume anything posted publicly, even under a fake name, can eventually be connected to you, whether through writing style, posting times, location metadata in photos, or who you follow and interact with. Strip metadata from every file before sharing it and be mindful of small details that can identify you.
- If you're an activist or journalist, sign up for Access Now's Digital Security Helpline. It offers free, direct help if you think you've been hacked or targeted.
