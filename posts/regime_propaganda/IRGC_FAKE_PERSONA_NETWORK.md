# IRGC ran a 62-account network posing as Texans, Scots, and Chileans for years, then pivoted to war propaganda overnight

Clemson University's Media Forensics Hub published a report exposing a long-running, multilingual influence operation tied to Iran's islamic revolutionary guard corps (IRGC). The network spent months (in some cases years) building convincing local personas across the Americas and the British Isles, only to drop the act in early March 2026 and flood the same accounts with pro-regime war propaganda the moment the US and Israel struck.

## Findings from the report

- The IRGC built and maintained a new network of at least 61 accounts, faking identities across two continents at once: one set operated in Spanish pretending to be located in the Americas and one set operated in English pretending to be in the British Isles.
- The fake personas weren't generic. They specifically impersonated accounts claiming to be in Texas, California, Venezuela, and Chile, plus accounts claiming to be from Scotland, England, and Ireland. The regime operatives spent years pretending to be Texans and Scots online.
- The whole point of the deception, per the report itself: all these accounts systematically amplify politically divisive content and disinformation aligned with IRGC narratives, and they are designed to exploit regional fault lines to advance the islamic republic regime's interests. They build trust as "locals" specifically so they can later weaponize that trust.
- The researchers confirmed the network spent months (in some cases, years) cultivating local personas and amplifying divisive political issues before pivoting in early March to pro-regime propaganda surrounding the war involving Israel, the United States, and Iran, which was a years-long sleeper operation that activated the instant a real war started.
- This was not the network's first deployment. It had already been previously linked to campaigns that targeted the United Kingdom and promoted narratives around Scottish independence before being repurposed for war propaganda.
- The scale of just one platform alone is staggering: Clemson University located 47 X (previously Twitter) accounts, nine Instagram accounts, and five Bluesky accounts that fell into the IRGC "network", and X accounts alone created 59,403 original posts that were reposted thousands of times with organic engagement by thousands of followers, potentially reaching millions.
- While most accounts were less than a year old, some date back to December 2023, meaning the groundwork was laid more than two years before the regime needed to use it.
